## Agreed Target Behavior

### Independent Resource Lanes

- Energy and Magic each have their own ordered activity list. Apply the same separation to managed R3 where applicable; no operation gains permission over an unmanaged lane.
- For each lane, satisfy the highest active, eligible priority's defined demand/cap first. Only surplus can fund the next priority. Do not approximate this with huge weights or proportional splitting across priority levels.
- Reconcile the whole authorized lane, not just currently idle resources. A new high-priority demand can reclaim resources from lower priorities; removal returns resources to the current remaining priorities, not a saved allocation snapshot.
- Preserve weighted sharing within an activity where appropriate: Misc Energy NGU weights divide the Energy NGU allocation; Misc Magic NGU weights separately divide Magic NGU allocation. Confirmed zero exclusions remain exclusions.
- Unused lower priorities remain configured but dormant. Temporarily recalled allocations during application are not evidence that the desired activity disappeared.
- A hard gate, permission or safety dependency is not a soft priority that can be outweighed. Unavailable ordinary activities and blocked mandatory obligations need explicit eligibility rules; do not silently bypass a mandatory gate.

Example: Energy stays focused on Energy NGU. Magic normally goes to Magic NGU,
but active blood preparation takes higher Magic priority. If blood consumes the
entire Magic budget, Magic NGU receives zero and its Magic-NGU-specific gear/beard/
digger bonuses contribute zero utility. If blood is satisfied using only part of
the budget, the actual surplus can fund Magic NGU and make those benefits relevant.
When blood preparation ends, remove its request and recompute the Magic lane.

### Shared Setup Follows Effective Work

- Energy/Magic allocation order is separate from how shared gear, beards, diggers and gold budget trade off benefits across both lanes. Resolve that tradeoff explicitly; see questions below.
- Do not keep optimizing Magic NGU merely because its saved weight is positive when higher-priority Magic work leaves it no budget. The same rule applies symmetrically to every activity/lane.
- Evaluate prospective legal allocations for candidate setups, not only stale current allocations. A zero caused by an in-progress recall must not suppress a still-desired goal or prevent a setup from creating useful overflow.
- Capacity/rate/support changes can alter overflow. Selection and allocation must agree on which priorities can actually run, without oscillating between incompatible setups.
- Planned support may choose affordable replacements; keep-current and explicitly pinned choices must not be silently resized or converted to a different policy.

### One Execution Model

- Features emit baseline intent, compatible temporary claims and finite-action requests. One game-thread executor observes, resolves, applies necessary differences and verifies them.
- Distinguish uncontrolled, controlled-empty and controlled-with-a-value domains. No goal must not mean clear everything or install an implicit NGU fallback.
- A current successor supplies its desired setup directly. Remove historical restore-then-apply ladders. Keep narrow still-owned returns for borrowed native settings and any explicitly requested return baseline.
- Retain goals, acquired decisions, stage entry/credit/deadline, acquired AT targets, W/OS history and action identities. Application observations and caches are rebuildable; native progression is not rollback state.
- Known partial writes converge from verified progress. Rendering failure does not repeat committed accounting. Foreign changes or genuinely unknown outcomes stop only the affected/dependent unsafe operation, not unrelated healthy work.
- Purchases, spells, harvesting, pit feeds, fights and rebirth remain identified finite actions. Never repeat them merely because an executor reset or preparation conditions still hold.
- Keep expensive gear/support/route/phase selection event-driven. Cheap recurring allocation must not perform full inventory/forecast capture or replacement optimization.
- Preserve original stage-kind semantics: protected fixed time, actual mandatory AT completion, and applicable elapsed deadlines are different. Preflight failure/retry must not consume a stage, manufacture credit or grant extra time.
- No native references or old cleanup flags become durable write authority. Checkpoint and game-save rollback cannot provide a universal exactly-once guarantee.
